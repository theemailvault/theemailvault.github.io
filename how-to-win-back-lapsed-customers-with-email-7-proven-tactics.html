How to Win Back Lapsed Customers With Email (7 Proven Tactics)

2026-07-24

Your store probably has hundreds or thousands of customers who bought once, maybe twice, then disappeared. They're not gone forever—they're just dormant. The right email sequence can pull them back.

Winning back lapsed customers is often cheaper than acquiring new ones, and those recovered customers already know your brand. This guide walks you through the exact tactics that work: how to identify them, what to say, and how to structure your campaign for maximum response.

Who counts as a lapsed customer?

Before you build your win-back campaign, define what "lapsed" means for your store. This isn't universal.

For a subscription box service, lapsed might mean no purchase in 30 days. For a luxury goods store, it could be 12 months. For everyday consumables, it might be 60 days.

Look at your repeat purchase cycle. If your average customer buys every 45 days, someone inactive for 90 days is genuinely lapsed. If your average cycle is 200 days, you might not send win-back emails until month 7 or 8.

Use Shopify's built-in customer analytics or a tool like Klaviyo to pull a list of customers who:

  • Haven't purchased in [your defined period]
  • Have made at least one previous purchase
  • Are still actively subscribed to email
  • Have a valid email address

This segment is your starting point.

Segment by purchase history

Not all lapsed customers are equal. A customer who spent $500 once needs a different approach than one who spent $15.

Create at least three segments:

High-value lapsed customers. These are your VIPs. They spent significantly or bought multiple times. Send them to a special win-back sequence with a generous incentive (15-25% off, free shipping, exclusive access to new products). These emails should feel personal and acknowledge their past loyalty.

Regular lapsed customers. They made moderate purchases on a normal schedule but stopped. A 10-15% discount plus a clear reason to return ("We've added new sizes" or "Your favorite items are back in stock") works well here.

Low-value lapsed customers. One small purchase, then silence. You still want them back, but the offer might be lighter (free shipping, a smaller percentage discount, or a gift card). The cost of winning them back shouldn't exceed their likely lifetime value.

Sending a $20 discount code to someone who bought once for $18 doesn't math. Send them free shipping instead, or a 10% code. Save the aggressive offers for customers who've proven they'll spend more.

The win-back email sequence that works

A single email gets ignored. A sequence creates urgency and multiple chances to convert.

Here's a structure that generates results:

Email 1: The gentle reminder (send immediately)

Subject line: "We miss you, [First Name]"

This email is soft. No aggressive discounts yet. Its job is to remind them you exist and highlight what's new.

Example opening: "Hi Sarah, it's been a few months since you shopped with us. We've added 40 new styles to the collection you browsed last year, and we thought you'd want to see them first."

Include 3-4 product recommendations based on past purchase behavior or category interest. Link to your best-sellers. End with a gentle call-to-action: "Browse what's new" or "See our latest drop."

Email 2: The incentive email (send 4-5 days later)

Subject line: "Come back and save 15%"

Now introduce the discount. Be specific about the offer and the window. "Use code COMEBACK15 by [date] for 15% off your next order."

Mention a specific problem they might have experienced: "Didn't find what you needed last time?" or "Want to try a different size?" This addresses friction.

Include social proof. A customer review or testimonial can work. A simple line like "Join 2,000+ happy customers who trust us" costs nothing but builds confidence.

Email 3: The urgency email (send 4-5 days later)

Subject line: "Your 15% discount expires in 24 hours"

This is the final push. Emphasis on scarcity. Keep it short: remind them of the offer, show the code one more time, and explain the deadline.

Don't oversell. "Just making sure you didn't miss this" is honest and effective. If they don't convert here, they're probably not converting.

Email 4 (optional): The exit email (send 3-5 days after expiration)

Subject line: "One last offer inside"

If they still haven't converted, send a softer final attempt. Extend the deadline by 3-5 days or offer a smaller incentive (free shipping instead of the percentage off). Acknowledge the silence directly: "We'll always have a place for you if you change your mind."

This isn't aggressive. It's genuine. Then remove them from the win-back sequence and let them cool off for 30-60 days before trying again.

Tactics that lift conversion rates

Use dynamic content. Show products they've viewed or categories they've bought from before. Generic product grids don't work as well as personalized recommendations. If you bought winter boots in 2022, you want to know about new winter boots, not swimwear.

Acknowledge the gap. Don't pretend it hasn't been quiet. "It's been [number] months since your last order" or "We noticed you haven't visited us in a while" actually builds trust. It shows you remember them.

Make the offer time-limited. Open-ended discounts don't create urgency. "Use this code until Thursday" gets clicks. "Use this code anytime" gets ignored.

Test subject lines against each other. A/B test "We miss you" against "Your exclusive comeback offer" or "What you loved is back." Lapsed customers are less likely to open mail, so subject line matters more than for active segments.

Keep the copy short. Lapsed customers are skimming, not reading. One paragraph, one image, one clear button. Simplify.

Timing and frequency

Send your win-back sequence over 10-14 days total. Space emails 4-5 days apart. This cadence is frequent enough to create momentum but not so aggressive it damages your sender reputation.

If you're sending to a large list (10,000+ lapsed customers), stagger the send times. Send 25% on Monday morning, 25% on Tuesday morning, and so on. This prevents a spam trap spike and spreads server load.

Don't send win-back emails on Saturdays or Sundays unless your audience is known to engage on weekends. Weekdays, early morning (6-9 AM) or early afternoon (1-3 PM), consistently outperform other windows.

The math worth tracking

After you launch your win-back campaign, measure:

  • Open rate on email 1 (you should expect 20-35% for a cold segment)
  • Click-through rate on the incentive email (10-20% is solid)
  • Conversion rate on the offer (2-8% is typical for lapsed segments)
  • Revenue recovered divided by email sends (aim for at least 3-5 cents per email)

If your conversion rate is under 1%, adjust the offer or the segment. Maybe your discount isn't high enough. Maybe you're targeting the wrong people. Test one variable at a time.

Most win-back campaigns break even or turn profitable by email 3. After that, any conversions are bonus.

Start with your highest-value lapsed segment first. Prove the model works, then expand to regular and lower-value customers. Once you see what works, you can run this campaign quarterly and turn it into predictable revenue recovery.

5 Abandoned Cart Email Sequence Examples That Actually Convert

2026-07-01

Your customers add products to their carts and vanish. It happens to almost every Shopify store, and it's one of the easiest revenue leaks to plug. A well-designed abandoned cart email sequence can recover 10-30% of that lost revenue without spending a dime on new traffic.

The difference between a mediocre recovery sequence and one that works comes down to specificity: what you say, when you say it, and how you say it. Generic "don't forget your cart" messaging underperforms. Sequences built on psychology and real customer behavior move merchandise.

This article walks through five concrete abandoned cart email sequence examples you can adapt for your store today, plus the timing and structure that makes them work.

Why abandoned cart sequences matter

Abandoned carts represent genuine, high-intent traffic. These customers already loaded items into their bag. They didn't bounce from a homepage or scroll past your ads—they chose your products and came close to buying.

The average cart abandonment rate sits between 60-80% across ecommerce. That's not a failure of your store alone; it's the nature of online shopping. But it also means roughly 70 out of 100 customers who almost bought from you will never be prompted again unless you reach out first.

An automated email sequence that triggers when someone abandons their cart is the simplest, highest-ROI move you can make.

The structure that works: timing and psychology

Before diving into specific email copies, understand the backbone: most effective sequences run 3-4 emails over 72 hours.

Email 1 (sent 1 hour after abandonment): Urgency, zero friction. The customer is still warm. Show the exact products they left behind. Minimal copy.

Email 2 (sent 24 hours later): Address objections. Introduce a small incentive. Add social proof.

Email 3 (sent 48-72 hours later): Final push. Strongest incentive. Scarcity angle. Last chance framing.

Some stores add a fourth email 1-2 weeks later targeting only users who didn't convert, but most of your recovery happens in the first 48 hours.

Abandoned cart email sequence example 1: The straightforward reminder

This works for commodity or time-sensitive products (seasonal goods, limited stock).

Subject line: "You left [Product Name] in your cart"

Email body:

Hi [First Name],

You added [Product Name] to your cart—then left without checking out.

We just want to make sure you didn't change your mind. Your cart is waiting, and your items are still available.

[COMPLETE YOUR ORDER]

Your order total: $[Amount]

Still have questions? Reply to this email. We're here to help.

[Store Name]

Why it works: Speed. Brevity. Assumes they simply forgot. No pressure, no gimmicks. Best for stores with good products and fair pricing—if you have neither, a reminder alone won't save the sale.

Abandoned cart email sequence example 2: The curiosity angle with social proof

Use this when you have customer reviews, testimonials, or new products similar to what they abandoned.

Subject line: "See why others loved [Product Category]"

Email body:

Hi [First Name],

We noticed you were looking at our [Product Category]. Before you go, we wanted to share something.

[Customer quote about the product or category. Example: "I was skeptical until I tried it. Three months later, I'm ordering again. Best decision." - Sarah M., verified buyer]

Your cart still has: [Product 1] [Product 2]

We also just restocked [related product they'd like], which customers are loving for [specific benefit].

[COMPLETE YOUR ORDER OR BROWSE]

Shipping is free on orders over $50.

Best, [Store Name]

Why it works: Social proof lowers buyer hesitation. Mentioning a restock or related product keeps them engaged even if they don't immediately buy what's in their cart. The free shipping mention removes one friction point.

Abandoned cart email sequence example 3: The incentive sequence (email 2 in a longer flow)

Send this 24 hours after the first reminder. It introduces a small discount or free gift.

Subject line: "Complete your order + get 10% off"

Email body:

Hi [First Name],

We hate to see great customers leave empty-handed.

Your cart is worth $[Amount]. Use code COMEBACK10 at checkout to take 10% off your order—today only.

[CLAIM YOUR DISCOUNT & FINISH CHECKOUT]

Items in your cart: [Product with price] [Product with price]

New total after discount: $[New Amount]

This code expires at midnight tonight.

Thanks for shopping with us. [Store Name]

Why it works: Time-bound incentive creates urgency without feeling desperate. 10% is enough to tip an undecided customer without gutting margin. The specific expiration time (midnight) is more effective than vague language like "soon."

Abandoned cart email sequence example 4: The "we have a problem" angle

This works well for higher-ticket items or when the cart is sitting for 48+ hours. It's more conversational and plays on loss aversion.

Subject line: "We might lose your order"

Email body:

Hi [First Name],

Here's the thing: you added [Product] to your cart two days ago. It's still sitting there, but we can't guarantee stock much longer. We've had strong demand this week.

Before it sells out, we wanted to flag it.

Your cart: [Product - Stock status: Only 3 left] [Product - In stock]

Total: $[Amount]

If you want it, grab it now. If something didn't work out, reply back and let us know. We're happy to help or suggest something else.

[COMPLETE YOUR ORDER]

[Store Name]

Why it works: Addresses real scarcity (especially if true). Gives the customer an "out" by inviting conversation. Feels less salesy because it's honest. Best used only when you actually have limited stock.

Abandoned cart email sequence example 5: The final sequence email (72 hours)

This is your last touch before they drop off. Use your strongest incentive or framing here.

Subject line: "This offer ends tonight"

Email body:

Hi [First Name],

Last chance.

You had this in your cart: [Product - $Price] [Product - $Price]

We're offering free shipping on your order through tonight (code FINALCHANCE covers it). After that, the offer resets.

[FREE SHIPPING - CLAIM CODE & CHECKOUT]

Can't decide? We've got a 30-day return window, so there's basically zero risk. Thousands of customers have already taken us up on this.

Final total: $[Amount] (before free shipping)

Let's make this happen.

[Store Name]

Why it works: Final email uses the strongest incentive (free shipping often converts better than percentage discounts for mid-to-high-ticket items). Removes return anxiety with an explicit policy. Urgency is honest—it's literally your last outreach.

Common mistakes to avoid

Don't assume one sequence fits every customer. If someone abandoned a $15 item, a 4-email barrage feels aggressive. If they abandoned $300 in products, a single email under-invests.

Don't make the copy about you ("We miss you!" or "Come back!"). Make it about them and their benefit.

Don't hide the products or pricing. Lead with what they actually left behind. Curiosity doesn't work here; clarity does.

Don't send from a noreply address. That makes it feel automated and impersonal, even though it is. Sign it with a real person's name when possible.

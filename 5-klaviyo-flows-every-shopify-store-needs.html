5 Klaviyo Flows Every Shopify Store Needs

2026-07-21

If you're running a Shopify store without automated email flows in Klaviyo, you're leaving money on the table every single day. The right flows handle your best-converting messages automatically while you sleep, work on other parts of your business, or scale customer acquisition.

This guide covers the five flows that consistently deliver results for Shopify stores: abandoned cart recovery, post-purchase follow-up, win-back campaigns, VIP repeat customer nurture, and product review requests. Each one has a clear job. Each one pays for itself.

1. Abandoned Cart Flow (The Revenue Saver)

An abandoned cart flow is non-negotiable. Customers add products to their cart, leave your site, and forget about the purchase. Email brings them back.

Most stores see 20-40% of abandoned carts recovered through email alone. The sequence works best with three touches:

Email 1: Reminder (sent 1 hour after abandonment) Keep it short and straightforward. Remind them what they left behind, show a product image, and make checkout easy with a direct link. No heavy discounting yet. Example subject: "You left something behind at [Store Name]"

Email 2: Sweetener (sent 24 hours after Email 1) Now you can offer incentive. A small discount (10-15% for most product types) or free shipping removes final hesitation. Example subject: "Complete your order - here's 10% off"

Email 3: Final push (sent 48 hours after Email 2) Create urgency without being pushy. Mention limited inventory or that the item is popular. Example subject: "[Product Name] is almost gone"

In Klaviyo, trigger this flow on the "Abandoned Cart" event. Set delays between emails, and make sure to exclude anyone who completes their purchase (so they don't keep getting reminder emails).

2. Post-Purchase Sequence (The Trust Builder)

A post-purchase flow turns first-time buyers into repeat customers. This flow starts immediately after purchase and typically includes 3-5 emails over 2-3 weeks.

Email 1: Order confirmation + thank you (send immediately) Confirm their purchase, show order number and tracking information, and thank them genuinely. This email should already be going out; use it as your flow's first trigger.

Email 2: Shipping + social proof (sent 2 days after purchase) Once the order ships, send tracking details. Include customer reviews, testimonials, or user-generated content for the product they just bought. This builds confidence while they wait.

Email 3: Product guidance (sent 5 days after purchase) Assume they've received it or are about to. Share tips on using the product, care instructions, or complementary items. Example: if they bought a coffee maker, suggest their favorite coffee subscription.

Email 4: Review request (sent 10 days after purchase) Ask for a review with a direct link to your review platform or Shopify product page. Keep it simple. Example subject: "Did you love it? Tell us"

Email 5: Related product offer (sent 14 days after purchase) Recommend a complementary product or a best-seller from your store. This is your second conversion opportunity while their buying mindset is warm.

Trigger this on "Placed Order" and exclude anyone who has already made a second purchase in your store (they're already repeat customers).

3. Win-Back Campaign (The Re-engagement Flow)

Customers who bought from you once but haven't returned eventually forget you exist. A win-back flow reminds them and offers incentive to shop again.

Run this flow on a monthly segment: customers who haven't purchased in 90+ days (or whatever timeframe makes sense for your business).

Email 1: "We miss you" (send immediately to segment) Make it personal and genuine. Reference something they bought before. Example subject: "We've got new [category they bought in before] you'll love"

Email 2: Exclusive offer (sent 3 days later) Give them 15-20% off or a specific product recommendation. Make it feel like a VIP thing, not a desperate clearance sale. Example subject: "A special offer, just for you"

Email 3: Showcase bestsellers (sent 7 days later) Show what's popular now. New products, top reviews, seasonal items. This is less about pushing a discount and more about sparking interest. Example subject: "Here's what everyone's buying right now"

4. VIP Repeat Customer Nurture (The Loyalty Builder)

Once someone has bought twice or more, they're valuable. Treat them like it.

Create a flow triggered on "Made 2nd Purchase" or use a segment of customers with 2+ orders. Send them:

Email 1: VIP welcome (sent immediately) Acknowledge their loyalty. Offer early access to sales, exclusive discounts, or a special gift. Example subject: "Welcome to our VIP club - here's 15% off"

Email 2: Exclusive early sale access (sent monthly) Every time you run a promotion, let repeat customers shop 24 hours early. Trigger this manually or set up a separate series if your sales are predictable.

Email 3: Personalized recommendations (sent monthly) Based on their purchase history, recommend complementary products. If they bought a yoga mat, suggest blocks and straps.

This flow keeps repeat customers engaged and increases their lifetime value significantly.

5. Product Review and Feedback Flow (The Social Proof Builder)

Reviews are trust signals that convert browsers into buyers. Make asking for them automatic.

Trigger this flow on "Placed Order" but send it 10-14 days after purchase (after they've had time to receive and use the product).

Email 1: Review request (single email) Keep it simple. Explain why reviews matter to your small business. Link directly to your review platform. Example subject: "Quick question: love your [product]?"

Email 2: Survey follow-up (sent 7 days later, only if Email 1 goes unopened) If they didn't respond to the review request, ask for feedback in a different way. A short survey or open-ended question sometimes gets better response than a direct review link.

These emails don't drive immediate sales but compound over time as your product pages fill with authentic customer feedback.

Getting the Basics Right

A few tactical notes that matter across all flows:

Timing and frequency. Test the intervals between emails. A good rule: don't send more than one promotional email every 48 hours to the same customer. Respect inbox fatigue.

Segmentation. Make sure your conditions are specific. Abandoned cart flows should exclude anyone who already purchased. Win-back campaigns should exclude recent buyers. This prevents confusing or annoying people.

Mobile-first design. Most emails are opened on mobile. Keep subject lines under 50 characters, preview text clear, and call-to-action buttons large and above the fold.

A/B testing. Once flows are running, test subject lines, send times, and offers. Small improvements compound into significant revenue gains.

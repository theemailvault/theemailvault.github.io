Black Friday Email Marketing Calendar for Ecommerce: 7-Week Plan

2026-07-10

Black Friday is the single biggest revenue opportunity for most Shopify stores, but the emails that drive sales don't happen by accident. You need a structured Black Friday email marketing calendar that starts weeks in advance, builds momentum, and captures sales across every stage of the customer journey.

This guide walks you through a complete 7-week calendar, from teaser campaigns through post-BFCM follow-ups, with the exact timing and message types that work.

Why Black Friday email planning starts 7 weeks out

Most store owners wait until October 15th to think about Black Friday email. By then, you've already lost two critical weeks.

Your Black Friday email marketing calendar should begin in late September for a November sale. Here's why the timeline matters:

  • Early teaser emails build anticipation and warm your audience without immediately asking for the sale
  • Mid-period promotional emails create urgency and allow casual browsers time to act
  • Final push emails capture last-minute buyers
  • Post-BFCM emails recover abandoned carts and capitalize on the buying momentum

Starting early also gives you time to segment your list, test subject lines, and build out sequences without rushing.

The 7-week Black Friday email calendar breakdown

Week 1: List-building and foundational email (Late September)

Your first job is to grow your email list. Black Friday is when new subscribers join, so make it easy for them to enter the sale conversation.

What to send: - One brand or product education email to your existing list (no Black Friday mention yet) - Activate list-growth assets: add a pop-up offering 10-15% off in exchange for email signup - Set expectations: a single email saying "We'll have a Black Friday surprise coming" keeps people engaged without spoiling the reveal

Goal: Add 500-2,000 new subscribers depending on your traffic.

Week 2: Soft announcement and early-bird positioning (Mid-October)

Introduce Black Friday without heavy discounting.

What to send: - Email 1: "Here's what's coming on Black Friday" (no discount code yet) - Email 2: Early-bird offer for VIP subscribers or existing customers (optional, but effective)

Use this phase to gauge engagement. High open rates tell you which segments are primed; low engagement signals that your messaging needs adjustment.

Concrete example:

Subject line: "Black Friday is almost here. Here's your first look."

Body: Reference your best-selling products, hint at the discount percentage, and ask subscribers to check back. Don't send a code yet. This trains your audience to expect value without teaching them to ignore your emails until the discount arrives.

Week 3: Teaser sequence with countdown (Late October)

Build FOMO and remind people that Black Friday is real and soon.

What to send: - Email 1: "The discount is locked in. Here's what it covers." - Email 2: Customer testimonial or social proof email - Email 3: "48 hours to prepare" reminder

Space these 2-3 days apart. The goal is repeated touchpoints without overwhelming people.

Week 4: The final "sale live" announcement (Early November, sale day)

When your Black Friday sale goes live, email should be your first notification channel.

What to send: - Email 1: "It's live. [DISCOUNT CODE] is active now." Send this within 2 hours of going live. - Email 2 (same day, 4-6 hours later): A different angle. Focus on free shipping, bundle deals, or bestsellers instead of repeating the discount code.

Opening your sale with email ensures your most engaged audience sees it first. They buy faster, which generates early social proof and helps your site's conversion metrics.

Week 5: Mid-BFCM push emails (Throughout Black Friday and Cyber Monday)

Send 2-3 emails during the sale window. Don't oversend; two emails per day maximum.

What to send: - Email 1: "Best sellers are running low" or "These items are 80% of orders" - Email 2: "Last chance for free shipping" or "Ends tomorrow" - Cyber Monday specific: "Cyber Monday only" email with any new offer or angle

Use segmentation here. Customers who've already purchased don't need another discount code. Send them post-purchase content, gift guides, or bundle recommendations instead.

Week 6: Final push and deadline emails (End of sale)

This is where you capture the last-minute buyers.

What to send: - Email 1: "12 hours left" (subject line must convey urgency) - Email 2: "Sale ends tonight at midnight" - Email 3 (optional): "Sale extended by 1 day" if your data shows good engagement

The final email should go out 4-6 hours before the sale officially ends. After that, send a gentle "sale ended" email the next morning to those who didn't purchase, offering a consolation discount or waitlist for the next sale.

Week 7: Post-Black Friday sequence (Following week)

The work doesn't stop when Black Friday ends.

What to send: - Email 1: Abandoned cart recovery (send to anyone who added items but didn't buy during the sale) - Email 2: Thank you email to purchasers with product care, shipping, or upsell information - Email 3: "We still have limited stock" email (if inventory remains) - Email 4: Winback email to non-openers during the sale offering a smaller discount for second-chance buyers

Segmentation that actually moves the needle

Don't send the same email to new subscribers and 3-year customers.

Create these segments: - VIP / High-lifetime-value customers (send early offers, exclusive discounts) - Repeat purchasers (emphasize new products, exclusive bundles) - New subscribers (more educational, smaller initial discount) - Lapsed customers (stronger incentive to return) - Abandoned cart segment (from 3+ months ago, not just this week)

Each segment gets different subject lines, offers, and messaging. A first-time subscriber needs context; a loyal customer needs a reason to buy again.

Key metrics to track in your calendar

As you execute your email marketing calendar, measure these:

  • Open rates by email type. What subject lines work best for your audience?
  • Click-through rates and which links drive traffic. Better product pages improve conversions.
  • Conversion rates by segment. Tailor future campaigns based on what works.
  • Unsubscribe rates. A jump in week 5 means you're oversending.
  • Revenue per email. Track which message types generate the most revenue, not just opens.

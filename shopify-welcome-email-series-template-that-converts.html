Shopify Welcome Email Series Template That Converts

2026-07-07

A strong welcome email series is one of the fastest ways to earn trust and drive early revenue from new Shopify subscribers. When executed right, a 3-5 email sequence can deliver 30-50% of new customer revenue in the first 30 days. This guide walks you through a practical, tested template structure you can implement immediately.

Why welcome series matter for Shopify stores

New subscribers are in peak receptivity mode. They just joined your list because they're interested in what you sell. A welcome series capitalizes on that momentum by introducing your brand story, showing value, and removing friction to a first purchase.

Without a structured series, new subscribers disappear into your regular promotional rotation and you lose that early engagement window. With one, you set expectations, build credibility, and create a predictable revenue stream before someone ever sees a sales email.

The 5-email template structure

This sequence works across product categories (apparel, beauty, home goods, niche products). Space emails 1-2 days apart for the first three, then move to day 7 and day 14.

Email 1: The instant welcome (send immediately)

This goes out the second someone confirms their email. It should be short, warm, and set the stage.

Subject line: "Welcome to [Brand] - here's 15% off"

Body structure: - One-sentence brand hook (who you are, not what you sell) - A specific discount code with clear expiration (48 hours works) - One call-to-action button (Shop Now or Browse [Category]) - Optional: a brief line about what's coming next in their inbox

Example:

"Hi [First Name],

We create [your value prop] for people who [specific customer type].

Use code WELCOME15 for 15% off your first order (expires in 48 hours):

[SHOP NOW BUTTON]

Over the next week, we'll share product guides, customer stories, and more insider info.

Thanks for joining, [Founder name]"

Why this works: It removes the most common friction point (discount request), delivers instant value, and sets expectations about frequency. Keep it to 5-7 short lines max.

Email 2: Social proof and storytelling (day 2)

This email shifts from discount to trust-building. Use this to show real customer wins or tell a short brand origin story.

Subject line: "Why 12,000+ people love [product type]" or "How we started [brand]"

Body structure: - A customer testimonial (name, photo if you have it, short quote about results or fit) - Or a 2-3 sentence founder story (why you started the brand) - One product recommendation or feature highlight - Secondary CTA (optional): "Read more stories" or "Explore [category]"

This email typically gets lower click rates than Email 1. That's normal. Its job is authority, not conversion.

Email 3: Educational value (day 4)

This one solves a real problem your customers face, without selling directly. It could be a sizing guide, styling tips, ingredient breakdown, or a common question answered.

Subject line: "The one thing we hear from customers: [Problem]" or "[Product type] sizing: a guide"

Body structure: - Headline that speaks to a specific pain point - 3-5 short tips or a mini how-to (with subheadings) - One embedded image if relevant - Light CTA: "See products that solve this" or "Browse [category]"

Example for a skincare brand:

"Subject: The one thing we hear about combination skin (and how to fix it)

Hi [Name],

After reviewing 200+ customer messages, we kept seeing the same question:

'How do I manage oily skin AND dry patches?'

Here's what actually works:

  1. Cleanse once a day max (over-washing triggers oil production)
  2. Layer hydration before actives (actives on damp skin absorb better)
  3. Use different products for different zones if needed (don't force a one-size routine)

This is why our Hydra Serum works for combo skin types—it's lightweight and layerable.

[SHOP SERUMS]

More guides coming soon, [Founder]"

Email 4: Urgency + scarcity (day 7)

Return to conversion focus. Use this to highlight a limited-time offer, new arrival, or low-stock product.

Subject line: "Only 8 left in stock" or "[Product] ends Friday"

Body structure: - Reason for the offer (restock incoming, seasonal, sale event) - Product image and key benefit - Scarcity element (low inventory, limited time) - Clear CTA

Keep copy under 150 words. Scarcity works best when it's real, so only use this if stock or availability is actually limited.

Email 5: Community and retention (day 14)

This final email transitions the subscriber from "new" to "regular." Introduce a referral program, loyalty program, or exclusive community (Discord, exclusive content, etc.).

Subject line: "Invite a friend and get [reward]" or "Join our community"

Body structure: - Brief explanation of the perk - How it works (step-by-step) - Referral link or sign-up link - Explanation of what's next (they're now on regular email schedule)

Example:

"Subject: Tell a friend, get $20 in credit

Hi [Name],

Everyone you know probably has the same problem you did before you found us.

When you refer a friend, both of you get $20 off your next order (no limit).

[GET YOUR REFERRAL LINK]

You can now expect to hear from us every other week with new releases, guides, and member-only deals.

Thanks for being here, [Founder]"

Key metrics to watch

Track these for each email in your series:

  • Open rate: If under 20%, subject lines need work
  • Click rate: Expect 2-4% on promotional emails, 1-2% on educational
  • Unsubscribe rate: Spikes above 0.5% signal poor list quality or frequency fatigue
  • Revenue per email: Calculate by dividing revenue directly attributed to that email by number sent

A/B test subject lines (especially Email 1), send time based on your audience timezone, and adjust follow-up intervals if you see unsubscribe patterns.

Common mistakes to avoid

Discount stacking: Don't offer a better discount in Email 4 than Email 1. Once someone sees a better deal is coming, they'll wait.

Over-selling too early: Emails 2 and 3 should earn trust before asking for money again. Save hard sells for Email 4.

Unclear CTAs: One clear button per email outperforms multiple links. "Shop Now" beats "Click here for discounts and also join our community and follow us."

Ignoring unsubscribes: High unsubscribe rates mean your sequence is wrong for your list. Review copy tone and frequency before blaming your audience.

Neglecting mobile: Test every email on mobile. Most opens happen on phones. Short lines, large buttons, minimal images.
